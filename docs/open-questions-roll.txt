R-Q1  What happens to a resting order when a window closes?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : If our quotes are still resting when the window closes, what does
           the venue do - and what should we do?
Answer   : ANSWERED BY THE SPEC 2026-10-28, and it inverts the proposal R0 was
           written with.

           The venue cancels them itself. ExecRestatementReason(378) is
           "present on unsolicited cancels" and 2 = "Instrument lifecycle
           (closed/determined/paused)" (OE spec, ExecutionReport field table).
           So a resting order at the close terminates as an ordinary
           unsolicited ExecType=CANCELED(4) report carrying 378=2.

           Every layer already handles that, today, with no new code:
             - oe_decoder maps 378=2 to CancelReason::InstrumentLifecycle;
             - the reconciler halts on ExecType=REJECTED only, not on a
               cancel;
             - QuoteManager::on_report resets the quote on Canceled whether or
               not it was the one who asked.

           The danger is the opposite of what was assumed. Cancelling INTO the
           close is what breaks: if our cancel reaches the gateway after the
           lifecycle cancel has already terminated the order, the reply is
           OrderCancelReject with CxlRejReason(102)=1 UNKNOWN_ORDER - "an
           order that was already canceled ... reports UNKNOWN_ORDER(1)" - and
           any OrderCancelReject halts the daemon (OE-Q6). Leaving an order
           alone at the close is safe; racing the close with a cancel is not.

Built    : BUILT 2026-10-28, ahead of R2, because the default the operator
           asked for would otherwise have raced the venue at every close:
             - IStrategy gained abandon(), which drops the quotes and sends
               nothing; the host calls it instead of withdraw() when an
               instrument stops being tradable, and withdraw() everywhere
               else (operator stop, halt, withdraw-before);
             - CryptoMM gained the withdraw-before parameter, default 0;
             - cpm::md::parse_ticker reads the window end out of the
               instrument name, so the strategy can count back from it.
           So R2 does both halves of "cancel early, never cancel late":
           (a) withdraw --withdraw-before seconds ahead of the window end -
               now for a trading reason rather than a plumbing one, since the
               last seconds of a binary are where the informed flow is;
           (b) send no cancel at all inside that window, and none once Closed
               is seen. Anything still resting is the venue's to cancel, and
               it will.

Residual : One case survives: a fill in the final second can leave a new quote
           in flight that the lifecycle cancel then terminates, and our own
           cancel could still cross it. The spec says how to tell that case
           from a real one - "correlate against the ExecutionReports you have
           already received for that ClOrdID (a prior Canceled/Expired report
           means it had already terminated)" - and OrderState already holds
           exactly that. If R4 sees it happen, the narrow fix is: an
           OrderCancelReject of UNKNOWN_ORDER for an order we have already
           seen terminate is logged rather than halted. That is not a hole in
           fail-stop; it is the one case the spec itself defines as benign.
           Nothing else about the halt rule changes.
Status   : RESOLVED from the spec; R4 confirms the residual case at the venue.

R-Q2  How does the daemon learn the current window at startup?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : You will type BTCD15M. Which instrument is that, right now?
Evidence : It cannot be learned over FIX. The all-instrument lifecycle feed is
           changes-only: in O6 the subscription was sent at 11:17:17 and the
           first message arrived at 11:20:00, the next boundary. Per-symbol
           lifecycle behaves the same, which is why a status is Unknown until
           something changes ([MD] Q13). Neither fix-market-data.txt nor
           fix-order-entry.txt defines a SecurityList or any other "what is
           listed" request.
Options  : (a) WAIT. The strategy sits on its series doing nothing until the
               next window opens - at most 15 minutes for D15M, and it then
               starts at the beginning of a clean window rather than halfway
               through one. No new dependency, no reject risk, and the GUI
               says "waiting for the first window of BTCD15M".
           (b) DERIVE. The ticker is deterministic: series minus the D15M
               suffix gives the asset, the clock gives YYMMMDD and the next
               15-minute boundary gives HHMM, so BTCD15M at 06:07 UTC today
               is BTCD15M-BTC26OCT260615-0. Cheap and instant - but a wrong
               guess is an unknown-symbol reject, and that halts. It would
               need a narrow "this one subscription's reject is not a halt"
               exemption to be safe, which is the same kind of hole as
               R-Q1(b).
           (c) REST. Ask the venue for status=OPEN, which is how the O2
               example instrument was obtained. It is the correct answer and
               the expensive one: an HTTP client and REST authentication in a
               project whose dependency rule is QuickFIX and GoogleTest only.
Proposal : (a) WAIT, with (b) available later behind a flag if the wait turns
           out to annoy you in practice. A 15-minute series is not worth a new
           dependency or a hole in fail-stop to join half a window early.
Answer   : (c) REST, decided 2026-10-28 by the operator: typing BTCD15M must
           start quoting the current window, not the next one.
           What it takes, from the API spec:
             GET /api/v1/instruments?series_ticker=BTCD15M&status=OPEN
             headers CB-ACCESS-KEY / -SIGN / -PASSPHRASE / -TIMESTAMP
             sign = base64(HMAC-SHA256(ts + method + path)), query params
             excluded, empty body on a GET (spec 2.2) - which is exactly
             cpm::hmac_sha256_b64, already written for the FIX logon.
           Costs: an HTTPS GET client on the OpenSSL already linked (~250
           lines), and the JSON reader moved out of gui/ into shared/ so both
           can use it. No third-party dependency is added.
           Credentials live in creds-rest.env (CPM_REST_*), created
           2026-10-28; the key needs the MARKET_DATA permission.
           Called once per series at startup and on reconnect, never polled:
           REST says what is open now, the FIX lifecycle feed says what just
           changed. This reverses [MD] Q13 ("no REST"), which was decided for
           lifecycle state before anything needed to resolve a series.
           Bonus, and not a small one: the instrument object carries
           price_level_structure, so the tick grid stops being the
           --tick-structure guess that GUI-Q23 flagged and becomes something
           the venue states per instrument.
Proven   : 2026-10-28, against the sandbox, first attempt - HTTP 200 with the
           signature exactly as planned. Capture:
           docs/spec/capture-rest-20261028T1205-instruments.txt.
             BTCD15M -> BTCD15M-BTC26OCT281215-0  OPEN
             ETHD15M -> ETHD15M-ETH26OCT281215-0  OPEN
           Three things R1b must build to, learned from it:
             - ONE SERIES PER CALL. series_ticker=A&series_ticker=B is HTTP
               400; A,B is HTTP 200 with zero rows, read as one literal
               ticker. Two series, two calls.
             - THE WINDOW END COMES FOR FREE. expected_close_time and
               metadata.market.window_end_time both carry it, so
               withdraw-before does not need the ticker parsed when REST has
               answered. close_time is null while the instrument is open - it
               is the most recent transition to CLOSED, not the schedule.
               parse_ticker stays as the fallback and the cross-check.
             - THE OBJECT CARRIES MORE THAN THE TICKER. price_level_structure
               (GUI-Q23), min_order_size, contract_size,
               position_limit_per_instrument, speedbump_ms, open_interest,
               total_volume, and metadata.market.price_strike with the
               window's start and end. Several of those are risk inputs
               nothing else in this system can see.
Status   : ANSWERED and PROVEN. Nothing blocks R1b.

R-Q9  Should a bad symbol halt the daemon?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : Until rolling is built, typing BTCD15M into the symbol field used
           to return {"ok":true} and then halt the daemon four seconds later:
           "MarketDataRequestReject 281=0: unknown symbol=BTCD15M". A typo in
           a form field ended the trading session.
Built    : 2026-10-28. A name that could never be an instrument - no dash, so
           a bare series - is refused at create time with a message naming the
           shape a ticker has. Nothing is sent, nothing is half-built, and the
           daemon stays live. Verified against md_sim before and after.
Remaining: That catches the series name. It does NOT catch a typo in a real
           ticker (BTCD15M-BTC26OCT260631-0), because nothing on either FIX
           session can be asked whether a symbol exists - there is no
           SecurityList (R-Q2) - so the gateway's reject is still the first
           news, and it still halts.
Question : Should the FIRST subscription of a newly created strategy be a
 for you    probe: its reject refuses the create instead of halting the
           daemon?
           The case for: a reject for a subscription we just sent, for a
           symbol we hold no book and no orders on, says "that name does not
           exist" - it says nothing about the feed we are already trading, so
           halting everything is a strange response to a typo.
           The case against: amendment 4 says any reject halts, full stop, and
           every exception is a place where a real fault can hide. This one is
           narrow - one MDReqID, sent moments ago, still unanswered - but it
           is an exception.
           Not built either way. Say which you want.
Status   : PARTLY BUILT; the probe question is open.

R-Q3  A position row per window
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : OrderState keeps a position per symbol. A D15M series is 96 windows
           a day, so after a day of rolling the positions table has 96 rows
           per series, nearly all of them settled and closed.
Proposal : Keep every row in the daemon - they are the audit trail, and the
           settled ones carry the payout - but have the GUI show the current
           window's position by default, with the settled ones behind a
           "closed windows" toggle and a per-series total. Nothing is thrown
           away; the screen simply stops being a list of everything that ever
           happened.
Status   : DEFAULTED 2026-10-28.

R-Q4  What is a "target", and can it still be an instrument?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : If `symbol` becomes a series, does the explicit ticker still work?
Proposal : Yes. The parameter keeps its name and takes either: a series
           (`BTCD15M`, no dashes) rolls; an explicit ticker
           (`BTCD15M-BTC26OCT260630-0`) pins to that one window and behaves
           exactly as today, which is what a one-off test or a replay wants.
           The two are told apart by shape, and the schema's help says so.
Status   : DEFAULTED 2026-10-28.

R-Q5  Does arming survive a roll?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : GUI-Q13 made stopping disarm, so that a restart cannot resume
           trading on an old confirmation. Is a roll a restart?
Proposal : No - arming survives. A roll is the strategy doing what it was
           armed to do, not the operator restarting it, and an operator
           re-confirming every 15 minutes would stop watching the
           confirmation. The counters, the id and the claim survive with it.
           Stop, halt and the kill switch all still disarm.
Status   : DEFAULTED 2026-10-28.

R-Q6  Two strategies, one series
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : GUI-Q6 refuses two strategies on one symbol because the position is
           per symbol and could not be attributed between them. With rolling,
           the symbol changes - what is claimed?
Proposal : The target. Two strategies on BTCD15M are refused; BTCD15M and
           ETHD15M together are fine. An explicit ticker claims that ticker,
           and a series claims every window it will ever roll onto, so a
           series and an explicit ticker of the same series collide and the
           second is refused.
Status   : DEFAULTED 2026-10-28.

R-Q7  What if a window never opens?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : A series that stops rolling - an outage, a trading halt, the venue
           retiring it.
Proposal : Not an error, and not a halt. The strategy stays running with
           nothing to quote and the GUI says how long it has been waiting.
           A halt is for a feed that is lying to us, not for a market that is
           not there. If the daemon has heard nothing from a series for longer
           than --series-quiet (default 2 windows), the GUI says so loudly.
Status   : DEFAULTED 2026-10-28.

R-Q8  Per-symbol lifecycle subscriptions
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : The daemon currently opens a lifecycle subscription per symbol
           alongside the book. With one all-instrument lifecycle subscription,
           are they still needed?
Proposal : No - drop them. The all-instrument feed carries every status for
           every instrument, and overlapping subscriptions only duplicate
           messages ([MD] O7), which MarketState then has to de-duplicate.
           One subscription, opened at startup, closed at shutdown.
Status   : DEFAULTED 2026-10-28.

=============================================================================
C. THINGS R1-R2 MUST NOT FORGET
=============================================================================

R-N1  The withdrawal is scheduled, not reactive
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The window end is in the ticker, so the daemon must act on the clock rather
than wait to be told the market closed. Waiting for Closed is already too
late: at that point a cancel is a cancel into a closed market, which is the
whole of R-Q1.

R-N2  Unsubscribe on Closed, not on Settled
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
An instrument that closed ~2 minutes ago is "unknown symbol" ([MD] O6), and an
unsubscribe for it is a reject, and a reject halts. The old window's book
subscription has to go at Closed. Its status after that still arrives on the
all-instrument feed, which is one more reason R-Q8 drops the per-symbol one.

R-N3  Clocks
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The window end is UTC and comes from the venue's own naming; the daemon's
clock is its own. A few seconds of skew is the difference between withdrawing
before the close and cancelling into it, so --withdraw-before must be large
enough to cover the skew, and the daemon should log the gap it observes
between the ticker's end time and the Closed it actually sees. Two or three
rolls of that log is better evidence for the default than any guess made now.

R-N4  md_sim already rolls, which is most of R2's test rig
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
md_sim has --roll-secs and emits the sandbox's exact sequence: Closed,
Determined, then Listed and Open for the next window, and Settled after
settle_delay (sim/src/market.cpp). So R1 and R2 can be tested end to end in
seconds rather than at the venue's 15-minute cadence, and R4 at the sandbox
becomes a confirmation rather than the first time any of it runs.
Two gaps to close in R2's tests rather than assume:
  - the simulator rolls ONE instrument; the daemon must be watched rolling
    two series independently, as BTCD15M and ETHD15M do within 50 ms of each
    other in the roll capture;
  - the simulator's next-window naming must match the venue's ticker shape, or
    SeriesWatcher's parser is being tested against itself. R1 tests the parser
    against the tickers in the captures, not against md_sim.
