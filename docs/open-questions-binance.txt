A. ANSWERED BY THE OPERATOR, 2026-10-29
=============================================================================

B-Q1  How does the daemon get Binance prices?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : WebSocket, or poll the REST book ticker?
Answer   : WEBSOCKET, hand-rolled. `<symbol>@bookTicker` is pushed on every
           change to the best bid or ask, so the guard reacts in
           milliseconds. Polling at 250 ms would add a delay about as long
           as the window the guard exists to close, and would spend a
           request every 250 ms forever to do it.
           The cost is accepted: the instructions allow QuickFIX and
           GoogleTest and nothing else, so the client is written here - the
           RFC 6455 handshake, client masking, frames, ping/pong, close and
           reconnect, on the OpenSSL already linked. That is the same
           reasoning that produced `cpm::https_get` and the JSON reader
           rather than pulling in a library ([ROLL] R-Q2).

B-Q2  PricingMidMoveThreshold is "a number of ticks" - ticks of what?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : The underlying's tick, a percentage, or absolute price?
Answer   : BINANCE PRICE TICKS. SUPERSEDED IN PART BY B-Q17: the tick size
           now comes from the symbol mapping in config/binance.conf, not
           from exchangeInfo, and the venue is futures rather than spot, so
           a BTCUSDT tick is 0.1 and not 0.01.
           Flagged at the time and worth repeating: BTCUSDT ticks at 0.01,
           so the numbers are large - 2500 is a $25 move, 1000 is $10.
           If that reads wrong on screen the alternative is basis points of
           the mid, which is scale-free and survives BTC changing price
           level, and it is a one-line change to the schema: the column
           renames itself from the label.
Type     : Int, default 0, minimum 0. **0 means off**, so every strategy
           that does not set it behaves exactly as it does today.

B-Q3  After a breach, what counts as "our market moves"?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : What releases the hold and lets it quote again?
Answer   : THE CPM MID MOVES AT LEAST ONE TICK from where it was when we
           pulled the quotes. Not any book update - a size change at an
           unchanged price is not our market repricing - and no timeout.
           The consequence is accepted: if CPM never reprices, we stay out
           for the rest of that window. That is the protection working, not
           a failure of it.

B-Q4  What if the Binance feed is stale or disconnected?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : Keep quoting unprotected, stop quoting, or halt?
Answer   : WITHDRAW AND STAY OUT while it is stale; the daemon stays up and
           other strategies are untouched. No reference means no protection,
           and quoting unprotected is what this exists to prevent.
           Deliberately NOT a halt. Amendment 4's fail-stop is for the feed
           we trade on; handing a third party's public feed the power to end
           the session is a different and worse trade.
           Stale means nothing received for `--binance-stale-after`, default
           5 s. BTCUSDT's bookTicker updates many times a second, so five
           seconds of silence is a dead feed, not a quiet market. A thinner
           symbol would want a longer one, which is why it is a flag.

B-Q5  When is the check run?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : On the strategy's 250 ms pass, or on every Binance update?
Answer   : ON EVERY BINANCE UPDATE, and not dependent on the quoting market
           updating at all. The operator was explicit, and two things in the
           code had to change for it:
             - the main loop slept 250 ms between passes, so a breach waited
               up to a quarter second. The feed thread now writes one byte
               to a self-pipe that the loop's existing poll() watches, and a
               Binance tick runs a pass at once. The 250 ms stays as a floor.
             - `StrategyHost::drive()` skips a strategy with no book, so a
               guard inside `on_market` would not fire when our book had
               gone quiet or closed with our orders still resting - exactly
               when we are most exposed. The guard gets its own entry point,
               `IStrategy::on_reference`, which the host calls for every
               Running strategy regardless of book state.
           The Binance thread still touches no strategy state: it stores the
           latest mid and signals. Every strategy call stays on the tick
           thread, which is the invariant the host was built for.

B-Q6  Production Binance, or the demo/test endpoint?   *** NEEDS YOUR EYES ***
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : The instructions say sandbox only, never production. Does that
           rule reach a third party's public price feed?
Spec ref : Instructions, Constraints: "Use the sandbox/test environment
           ONLY, never production." Binance publishes
           `wss://stream.binance.com:9443` (production),
           `wss://demo-stream.binance.com` and
           `wss://stream.testnet.binance.vision`.
Reading  : That rule is about the venue we trade on and the credentials that
           reach it. This feed holds no key, signs nothing, sends nothing
           but a handshake and cannot place an order. Reading it is not
           trading on production.
Proposal : PRODUCTION market data, read-only, with the endpoint as
           configuration so the demo or testnet stream can be selected
           without a rebuild. The reason is that the testnet book is not the
           real market, and a guard tested against a fictional BTC price has
           not been tested.
           Say the word and the default flips to the testnet endpoint.
B4       : EXERCISED READ-ONLY 2026-10-29, 20 seconds against
           stream.binance.com:9443 and api.binance.com, to prove the
           handshake, the framing and exchangeInfo: 1405 updates, BTCUSDT
           and ETHUSDT both usable, tickSize 0.01 for each. No key, nothing
           signed, nothing sent but the handshake, and no order anywhere
           near it. `--binance-host`, `--binance-port` and `--binance-plain`
           move it; this is a default, not a decision taken for you.
Note     : This does not make the sandbox a good test of the guard. CPM's
           sandbox BTCD15M is simulated and does not track real BTC, so a
           real Binance feed against it will breach constantly. B4's honest
           test is `binance_sim` driving both sides; the real-Binance part
           proves the feed, the framing and the reconnect, not the tuning.

B-Q7  Which Binance symbol belongs to which series?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : BTCD15M quotes BTC. What says so?
Answer   : The asset, which `cpm::md::parse_ticker` already pulls out of the
           window's ticker ([ROLL] R1), through a configuration map:
           BTC -> BTCUSDT, ETH -> ETHUSDT, overridable per deployment.
           Not a second column: the operator asked for one, the mapping is
           the same for every strategy on a series, and a per-strategy
           override would be a way to point a BTC strategy at ETH by typing
           in a box.
           A symbol whose asset is not in the map has no reference, which by
           B-Q4 means it does not quote - so a missing mapping is loud at
           startup rather than silent at runtime.

B-Q8  Which Binance number is "the mid"?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : Midpoint of the touch, or the last trade?
Answer   : (best bid + best ask) / 2 from `bookTicker`. It is the stream
           designed for this - pushed on every change to either side - and a
           midpoint does not jump with the side a trade happened to hit.
           Last trade would make the guard fire on which way someone
           crossed, not on where the market is.
           Odd sums round down, consistently, in scaled integers.

B-Q9  What is the reference measured from?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : The mid when we quoted, or the mid when our book last moved?
Answer   : THE MID WHEN WE QUOTED, refreshed on every place and replace. So
           it always reads "how far has Binance gone since we priced",
           never a drift accumulated across several re-quotes.
           The two readings agree where it matters: after a breach we only
           re-quote when our market moves (B-Q3), so the reference taken
           then *is* the mid at our book's last move.

B-Q10 What if the guard wants to cancel during the close window?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : A breach inside `withdraw-before` seconds of the window ending.
Answer   : ABANDON, not cancel - the same thing the lifecycle path does. A
           cancel of ours racing the venue's own close-cancel comes back as
           an OrderCancelReject, and any reject halts the daemon ([ROLL]
           R-Q1, [OMS] OE-Q6). The venue cancels what is resting at the
           close anyway, so abandoning loses nothing.

B-Q11 Does the guard run while disarmed?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : A dry run has nothing in the market to cancel.
Answer   : YES, and it logs what it would have done, placing and cancelling
           nothing - like every other decision in a dry run. It is the only
           way to watch the threshold's behaviour on a live market before
           trusting it with orders.

B-Q12 How are Binance prices held?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : They arrive as decimal strings with up to 8 decimals.
Answer   : Scaled int64 at 1e-8, parsed from the string with the existing
           `parse_scaled`. Never a double, anywhere, which is the market
           data rule ([MD] design §4) and applies the same here. BTC at
           70,000 is 7e12, four orders of magnitude inside int64.

B-Q14 One connection, and streams that follow the strategies
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : "I want just one web socket connection, then suppose I start for
           BTCD15M, it should subscribe feed for BTC from binance, if I stop
           the strategy for BTC it should unsubscribe the feed from binance
           for BTC too."
Answer   : BUILT 2026-10-29. One connection for the life of the daemon, to
           `/stream` with nothing in the URL, and streams subscribed and
           unsubscribed on it as strategies start and stop:
             {"method":"SUBSCRIBE","params":["btcusdt@bookTicker"],"id":1}
             {"method":"UNSUBSCRIBE","params":["btcusdt@bookTicker"],"id":2}
           each answered {"result":null,"id":N}. Confirmed against the
           venue's documentation and then against the venue itself.
           **Declared, not counted.** `ReferenceFeed::want(symbols)` takes
           the whole set that should be up and the reader thread sends the
           difference. The trader recomputes that set every pass from the
           host's own list - every strategy in state Running, mapped
           through B-Q7. There is no reference count to get wrong, a
           strategy that disappears however it disappears takes its stream
           with it, and a reconnect is just "want everything again".
           **Running, not created.** A strategy that exists but is stopped
           has nothing resting and is not quoting, so it needs no price.
           Start Feed subscribes the CPM book and not this one.
           **Regardless of the threshold.** A strategy with the guard off
           still subscribes. The alternative is for the daemon to read a
           strategy's parameters and decide what they mean, which is the
           one thing design-gui.md section 4 says it must not do; one
           unused stream of a possible 1024 is the cheaper mistake.
           **Inside the venue's budget.** Binance allows FIVE incoming
           messages a second on a connection and counts our pongs among
           them; an IP repeatedly disconnected for exceeding it may be
           banned. So: at most two control messages a second
           (`control_per_sec`), and N symbols cost one message rather than
           N because `params` is an array. An operator mashing start and
           stop cannot get the IP banned, and a test holds that.
           A price for a stream that has been dropped is discarded, and the
           mid goes with the subscription: a message already in flight when
           the UNSUBSCRIBE went out is not a reason to resurrect a stream
           nobody is listening to, and a mid nobody is maintaining is not
           stale, it is gone.
           `--binance-on` holds the connection open with nothing pinned,
           which is the usual way to run it. `--binance SYMBOL` additionally
           pins a stream for the whole run, whatever the strategies do.
Proven   : Against the simulator - SUBSCRIBE on Start, UNSUBSCRIBE on Stop,
           SUBSCRIBE again on restart, one connection accepted throughout -
           and against production Binance: a bare connection, BTC added,
           ETH added, BTC dropped, ETH dropped, with the mids appearing and
           disappearing to match.

B-Q15 Where do the URLs live?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : "I want a binance_config file to have all the urls so that I
           don't need to make changes in the code."
Answer   : BUILT 2026-10-29. `config/binance.conf`, read at startup from
           <dict-dir>/binance.conf or from wherever `--binance-config`
           says. Everything that was a literal in the source is a line in
           it: stream.host, stream.port, stream.tls, stream.path,
           rest.host, rest.path, ca_file, the timings, the asset-to-symbol
           map, pinned streams and tick-size overrides. Moving to the
           testnet, to a proxy or to the simulator is an edit and a
           restart, not a rebuild.
           **Order: built-in defaults, then the file, then the flags.** An
           operator who has set the venue once should not repeat it on
           every command line, and one overriding it for a single run
           should not have to edit the file.
           **An unknown key is an error, not a shrug.** `stream.hostname`
           stops the daemon with "unknown setting stream.hostname (line
           2)" and exit 78. A key that is quietly ignored is a setting that
           quietly does not apply, and the operator then believes the
           daemon is pointed somewhere it is not - which is exactly how the
           creds-rest.env that was never sourced went unnoticed for a whole
           session.
           A missing file at the DEFAULT path is not an error: it means the
           built-in defaults, which are production Binance, and the daemon
           says which of the two it used. A missing file named with
           `--binance-config` is an error.
           **No credentials, ever.** This feed signs nothing and the venue
           asks it for nothing. Keys stay in creds*.env in the environment,
           as the instructions require, and the file says so at the top.
           A test parses the shipped `config/binance.conf`, so a comment in
           it that is not a comment is found there rather than at startup.

B-Q16 When is the tick size looked up?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : The one REST call this project makes to Binance - exchangeInfo
           for PRICE_FILTER.tickSize - ran once at startup, over the
           PINNED symbol list. B-Q14 then made streams arrive on demand.
Answer   : FIXED 2026-10-29, and it was a real bug, not a tidy-up. A symbol
           subscribed after startup never got a tick size, usable() stayed
           false for it and the guard refused to quote - so with the
           shipped config (map.BTC, no pin, no tick override) and
           --binance-on, THE SPEED BUMP DID NOT WORK AT ALL. Every run of
           B3 and B4 missed it because each one pre-seeded the size with
           --binance-tick or tick.BTCUSDT.
           The lookup now happens where the subscription does, on the
           reader thread, for any wanted symbol whose size is unknown:
             - at most one attempt a minute per symbol, so a venue that
               refuses once is not asked again on the next pass, which
               would turn a missing tick size into a rate-limit ban;
             - a 5 second timeout, because this runs on the thread that
               answers pings and a ping unanswered for a minute costs the
               connection;
             - outside the lock, so nothing reading a price waits on it.
           The startup loop is gone: there is one path, and the pinned
           symbols take it like everything else. The daemon no longer
           blocks on a third party's REST endpoint before its FIX sessions
           log on.
           Two tests hold it: one wants a symbol that was never pinned and
           fails if nothing asks for its tick size (it fails against the
           old code, which was checked), and one that the retry is once a
           minute rather than once a pass. Proven against production with
           the shipped config: nothing pinned, nothing overridden, tick
           0.01 fetched when the symbol was first wanted, usable=yes.

B-Q17 No REST at all, and the tick size comes from the mapping
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : "First of all I don't want to use binance rest, second the
           binance config should not be this large, just want websocket url
           which is wss://fstream.binance.com/ws for now, second the symbol
           mapping, when we will do symbol mapping, there we can specify
           the tick size also like map.BTCD15M:{BTCUSDT, "tickSize":0.1}".
Answer   : BUILT 2026-10-29. Three changes, and they reverse parts of
           B-Q2, B-Q15 and B-Q16.
           **No REST.** `fetch_tick_size`, exchangeInfo, `rest.host`,
           `rest.path` and the lazy lookup of B-Q16 are all gone. This
           project now speaks to Binance over exactly one protocol, on
           exactly one connection. The bug B-Q16 fixed cannot recur because
           the code that had it no longer exists.
           **The venue is one line.** `url = wss://fstream.binance.com/ws`,
           taken apart by `parse_ws_url` into scheme, host, port and path:
           wss defaults to 443 and ws to 80, and the path defaults to /ws.
           The config file went from forty-odd lines to four settings.
           **The tick size travels with the mapping**, as the operator
           wrote it:
             map.BTCD15M = { BTCUSDT, tickSize: 0.1 }
           Braces, quotes and `:` versus `=` are all optional. The key is
           matched against a ticker's series and its asset, so either will
           do. A tick size is REQUIRED: with no REST to fall back on, a
           mapping without one would silently disable the guard for that
           instrument, which is exactly the failure B-Q16 was. Two
           instruments giving one Binance symbol different tick sizes is an
           error, not an override: a tick size belongs to the symbol.
           There are no built-in mappings any more. An empty map is warned
           about loudly at startup, because the only other symptom is a
           strategy that never quotes.
*** THE UNIT CHANGED ***
           fstream is the USD-M FUTURES venue, and BTCUSDT quotes there to
           two decimals - 80162.60 / 80162.70 - so a tick is 0.1, not
           spot's 0.01. **PricingMidMoveThreshold = 2500 is now a $250
           move, where on spot it was $25.** Every number in B-Q2 and in
           the B4 measurements is off by a factor of ten against this
           venue. Divide by ten, or raise the threshold.
Proven   : Against the venue itself, before any of it was written:
           wss://fstream.binance.com/ws accepts SUBSCRIBE and answers
           {"result":null,"id":1}; its bookTicker is raw, with the extra
           futures fields e/ps/T/E/st that the parser ignores; and
           LIST_SUBSCRIPTIONS confirms an UNSUBSCRIBE took effect.
           Then with the shipped config and nothing else: connected,
           subscribed, mid 80133.85, tick 0.1, usable.
Observed : On fstream, DATA KEEPS ARRIVING FOR SEVERAL SECONDS AFTER AN
           UNSUBSCRIBE - 185 more bookTicker messages over six seconds in
           one run - and the {"result":null} answer arrives after them,
           queued behind the data. The feed already discards a tick whose
           symbol it has dropped (B-Q14), which turns out to be load-
           bearing against the real venue rather than mere tidiness.

B-Q13 What happens across a reconnect?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question : Binance closes every connection at 24 hours, and may drop one at
           any time.
Answer   : The feed reconnects on a backoff and the mid simply goes stale
           while it is away, which by B-Q4 means the strategies withdraw and
           wait. A reconnect is therefore not a special case in the
           strategy, and a 24-hour disconnect looks exactly like a short
           network blip: quotes come out, then go back in.
           On reconnect the first bookTicker message is the current touch,
           so there is nothing to replay and no gap to detect - unlike the
           CPM feed, this stream carries no sequence to lose.
